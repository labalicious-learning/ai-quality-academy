# Teaching design and pilot plan

Session 00 adds two hours of setup to twelve two-hour learning sessions: 26 contact hours total. This is a foundation course followed by supervised apprenticeship, not a guarantee of expert performance.

Use one or two sessions per week with two to three hours of Product Studio homework between sessions, roughly 25–35 hours overall including presentation preparation. Adjust scope and support for learner circumstances. Start with an ungraded recall conversation before opening AI; revisit the concept in a new context. Demonstrate a worked example, then remove scaffolding gradually. These are the instructional choices for this pilot, not a claim that this precise schedule is experimentally optimal.

Core pattern: short demonstration, paired practice, break, independent task, peer critique, individual exit ticket. Ask learners to predict before executing a tool. After AI assistance, require them to explain the result from evidence without reading the chat verbatim.

Provide written instructions, captions for any instructor recording, keyboard operation, flexible pair roles and a low-bandwidth export path. Offer equivalent business themes; avoid assuming event-planning knowledge, gaming interest, or prior coding. No public leaderboard by bug count. Recognize clear reasoning, good questions, useful tests and collaborative review.

The individual [course project](COURSE_PROJECT.md) is the assessment: Session 01 discussion, Session 02 approved kickoff, regular reviews and a final presentation to Jared Cluff. No separate exams, hidden defense or numeric completion threshold. Keep software tests, evidence and learner explanation central; use gaps to plan coached revisions. Up to six presentations fit the Session 12 two-hour plan; larger cohorts need additional Jared-attended blocks. Pilot with 6–10 learners, record activity completion times and misconceptions, and adjust crowded lessons. Lesson 3's business surfaces are a sampler: repeat the sprint with different artifacts and approved connected work during apprenticeship.

Apprenticeship progression: supervised reproduction → own a low-risk charter → maintain a regression check → investigate integration failures → contribute to model evaluation. Grant responsibility based on observed performance and reviewer trust. Top-tier development requires sustained domain work beyond this course.
